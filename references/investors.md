# Big investors to learn from (US & India, last ~30 years)

Picked because they have been in the financial news repeatedly over decades and their holdings can be looked up. **Holdings are always looked up fresh** (filings lag: US 13F up to 45 days after quarter end; Indian shareholding patterns quarterly) — never quote holdings from memory.

## How to look up holdings

- **US 13F filings** (funds with >$100M in US equities, quarterly): SEC EDGAR full-text search (efts.sec.gov / sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar, form 13F-HR), or summaries at dataroma.com and whalewisdom.com. 13F shows long US stock and option positions only — no shorts, no cash, no foreign stocks.
- **ARK Invest**: publishes daily trades and full holdings on ark-funds.com.
- **India**: quarterly shareholding patterns on NSE/BSE list public holders with ≥1% of a company. Trendlyne "Superstar portfolios" and similar sites summarise them. Bulk/block deal data on NSE/BSE shows large trades the day they happen.
- **News**: WebSearch "<investor> new stake" / "<investor> sold" for recent moves.

## How to turn this into learning
1. Look up recent buys/sells. Note the date the trade was made vs when it became public (lag).
2. Ask *why* — the thesis — from news or letters (Berkshire letters, Pershing letters, interviews).
3. Check fit for a small account: long-term investors' picks belong in the **long-term** bucket, not in day/swing trades; small caps in India can be illiquid.
4. If a pattern repeats (e.g. "superstar small-cap buys after a 40% fall did well/badly"), record it in `lessons.md` as a hypothesis and test it.
5. Never copy blindly: by the time a filing is public, the price may already reflect it.
